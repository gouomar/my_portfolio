## The problem

Hubs hold a limited number of drones, edges carry a limited number per turn, and restricted zones take two turns to cross. Send every drone at once and you get gridlock; send them one by one and you waste turns. It looks like pathfinding, but it’s a scheduling problem.

## What I built

A command-line tool that reads a small map format and prints a turn-by-turn schedule, with an optional 3D replay. Each drone is planned with A* over space and time, and its route is stamped into a shared reservation table that every later drone has to respect.

## The thinking

Planning all drones together explodes, because the search space is the product of every drone’s options. Planning them one at a time, against the reservations of those before, keeps it tractable. The side effect: when two drones want the same corridor, the second shifts back one turn and a convoy forms without any code asking for it.

## What I learned

The heuristic decides whether A* is fast. Straight-line distance is the wrong guide when one edge costs two turns, so a backwards Dijkstra from the goal computes the real turn cost from every hub before any drone moves.

## What I’d do differently

Planning one drone at a time is greedy, not optimal. An early drone can box in a later one. I’d add a re-plan step that reshuffles the order when that happens.
