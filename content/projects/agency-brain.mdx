## The problem

Students message the agency on WhatsApp all day, in three languages and a romanized dialect. Routing rested on two cheap heuristics: a keyword regex for greetings and one similarity score for “is this in scope”. Both broke on real messages: “slm” slipped past the regex, and Arabic questions scored too low and got handed off.

## What I built

Two small local classifiers replace the two heuristics. Their output is locked to a fixed answer at generation time, so the router always gets a decision it can act on. Gemini only runs once a message has passed both gates.

## The thinking

The decision that mattered wasn’t which model writes the answers. It was seeing that routing doesn’t need generation at all. Treating “should this reach the LLM?” as a classification problem, and checking the retrieved evidence before paying for an answer, moved accuracy and cost in the same direction.

## What I learned

On a yes/no gate, constrained decoding changed nothing I could measure: the model already answered cleanly. Its payoff shows up the moment the output has structure. Free decoding produced usable JSON 39% of the time; constrained decoding, 100%, with no parsing code.

## What I’d do differently

The scope gate takes about 7 s per uncertain message on CPU. That’s fine at WhatsApp pace, but a smaller distilled judge would cut it. I’d also fold the hand-written “active conversation” rule into the classifier, so one decision covers it.
