## The problem

N coders sit around a table with N dongles, and each needs both neighbouring dongles to compile. Underneath, it’s the classic concurrency problem: one coordination mistake and every thread freezes, or one silently starves.

## What I built

Each coder is a POSIX thread, and each dongle is a mutex with a condition variable. Waiting threads sleep instead of spinning. A min-heap I wrote from scratch picks who gets a freed dongle (first come, or closest to burnout), and a separate monitor thread stops the run the moment anyone burns out.

## The thinking

The deadlock fix is asymmetry, not more locks. If every thread reaches left first, all of them can hold one dongle and wait forever. Making even threads reach right first breaks that cycle, so at least one thread always gets both.

## What I learned

A thread asleep on a lock can’t notice its own deadline. Detection has to live outside the threads it watches. That’s why the monitor is its own thread with nothing else to do.

## What I’d do differently

Timing uses `gettimeofday`, which jumps if the system clock is adjusted mid-run. I’d switch to `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, and choose the scheduler at runtime instead of at compile time.
